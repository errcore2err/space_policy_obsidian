#### [[Sputnik]] Memorandum
- Apparently there was a test of a Redstone rocket (Jupiter-C RS27 ICBM) in September 1956, which could have ignited it's final stage (which could have put aritifical satellite in orbit BEFORE sputnik) 
	- Feeling was that it was better to divorce the orbiting satellite idea from military testing, stressing peaceful nature
	- Russians had "done us a good turn" by establishig the concept of freedom of international space (orbital space)
- Looking ahead 5 years, what about a recon vehicle? Already an air force project underway (WHAT PROJECT?)
- Apparently there was a soviet request to put some US instruments onboard--this was not done due to the fact it would give them technological information
- ICBM testing and technology intimately related to satellite/space programs 
- Orbit inclinations are also tied (at the time) to launch points

- Memo to the president (Eisenhower) from Secretary Quarles regarding the launch of the Apollo program. Outlines the timeline 
	- A little bit of coping by saying that both cold war objectives (see [[Sputnik]] objectives) would be met by going as fast as possible with development of sputnik and also military rocketry [[ICBM|ICBMs,]] whereas the US was taking care not to slow down their own ICBM programs under development 

#### Colored [[Sputnik]] Article 
- Some historical points about the time of sputnik launch (1957):
	- 3 years after Brown V. Board of Ed, Eisenhower sending in 101st Airborne to Little Rock
	- No Americans fighting abroad, but many stationed in cold war hot zones
- If the Russians could launch a satellite like sputnik, they could just as easily launch a missile to DC and America needed the same capability 
- In reality, the US was winning both arms and space race
	- While
- [[Werner von Braun]] advocated for years to make satellites a priority, and proposed 1954 memo "A Minimum Satellite Vehicle" ([[Extra Readings#Week 2]]) to get something in space by 56 
- As part of the finger pointing in the aftermath of the launch, one of the points that came up was the Soviets might have been producing (educating) better scientists than the United States and blamed the education of kids 
	- Eisenhower backed the National Defense Education Act the next year, pointing to idea that education had become matter of national defense 
- Accelerated [[Vanguard]] program was on an accelerated schedule to respond to the sputnik launch (and failing attempts to shrug it off)
	- December 6, failed Vanguard launch

#### The Heavens and the Earth, pp  112-230
- Military power is an essential component of political comptetition
- Whether or nor the Soviets WOULD invade the west, their ability to do so, development of nuclear weapons, etc., influenced other countries views
- Especially after Korean war, it became clear that the US could not respond to communist expansion everywhere, but still required strategic superiority and a public willingness to use it as "big stick" 
- Became extremely important not to overspend into bankruptcy, and this required better intelligence about the soviets (based on the [[Extra Readings#Week 2|Killian Report]])
- Things like U2 spy planes could be used, but "blatant violations of USSR airspace" were very risky--better to have cameras which could be placed on satellites in space (where no concept of airspace existed)
- Von Braun's "Minimum Satellite vehicle" asked for only 100k, small amount given the potential huge impact
	- Would be considered a massive blow to national prestige and views of US science and engineering abroad
- When chooisng which rocket to use to launch the US satellite, it became clear that being first was not necessarily the main point: having as much distance as possible from military projects was higher priority
	- NRL rocket chosen, while having th have several stages designed from scratch, was initially designed for scientific mission(s)
	- By this point, there was still the question of legality of satellites overflying
- Eisenhower believed that military spending was not a spur to the economy but rather a dead loss
- Even after the US launched their own satellite program, there was still considerable questions about who should control space activities, and how to balance the military and civil space aspects
	- Page 170: "Committee members cannot comete with scientists at their own field. So we stay in our field--the objective. " What ought to be the objectives of a US space program? Propaganda, military power, science?
- Page 170: PSAC identified two distinct objectives: exploration and control
	- Military importance of surveillance, meteorology, and communications--these also raised questions about where space begins, how to allocate radio frequencies, etc. 
- Page 171: apart from recon satellites, major goals of spaceflight in the near term were scientific and political
- Division between civil/scientific and military applications a big deal

#### Sputnik and US Intelligence: The Warning Record
- Soviets achieved a political and propaganda triumph because Eisenhower believed a rush into space was unwarranted--for him, there was no "space race"
- Sputnik itself was not a threat, but what it represented was (closer towards ICBMs)
